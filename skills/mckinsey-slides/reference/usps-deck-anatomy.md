# USPS Future Business Model — deck anatomy

Source: `temp-mckinsey/McKinsey-USPS-Future-Business-Model-.pdf`, March 2, 2010,
39 pages. A genuine McKinsey client deck — slide-shaped, not a prose report — so
it is the best available evidence of how McKinsey actually builds slides.

Everything below is quoted verbatim from the document.

**Topic-label titles appear only in the reference annex** (slides 29, 32–36):
`Definition of non-legislative and legislative change`, `Products and services
opportunities for USPS`, `Pricing opportunities for USPS`, `Service level
opportunities for USPS`, `Workforce opportunities for USPS`, `Public policy
considerations`. Never in the argument.

### Grammar

- **14–20 words**, median ≈ 17. Argument titles under 12 words are rare.
- **No terminal punctuation, ever.** Internal commas used freely.
- **Sentence case.** Only proper nouns and defined case names capitalise:
  `First-Class Mail`, `"Base Case"`, `"Fundamental Change"`, `Standard Mail`.
- **Tense is section-disciplined**: diagnosis = present perfect (`have been
  driven`, `has exacerbated`, `have been significant`); forecast = `will`;
  recommendation = `can` / `will need to` / `will be required`.
- **~35% carry a number**, and the number is the payload, not decoration.

### Two signature constructions

**Concession, then punch** — concede the good news first, land the bad news second:

> Recent reductions in workforce usage have been significant, **but** pieces per FTE still declined in 2009

> The USPS has been responsive to declining volume, **but** recent work hour reductions will become increasingly difficult to replicate

**Front-loaded conditioner** — the qualifier before the claim:

> **Without aggressive management cost-cutting,** work hours will remain flat…

> **While payments in 2017-2020 drop,** RHB funding will continue…

> **In the short run,** USPS will violate its statutory financing requirements in October 2010

### There is no bold-takeaway-plus-clarifier pair

Titles are ONE sentence wrapping to exactly two lines, broken at a clause
boundary. The clarifier role is played by the kicker above and the chart
title + unit line below.

## 2. Slide anatomy

**Top-left kicker**, `Section` or `Section: Sub-scope`:
`Recent Context` · `Base Case` · `Base Case: Volume Declines` ·
`Base Case: Revenue Projection` · `Base Case: Fixed Cost of USO` ·
`Actions within Postal Service control: Productivity` ·
`Fundamental Change: Pricing`. Pivot slides (21, 38) deliberately have none.

**Top-right chip** — a repeated taxonomy (`Non-legislative` / `Legislative`) or
a chart legend.

**Numeric badge** left of the title (`1`, `2`, `3/4`) tying a drill-down back to
a master list.

**Body**: dominant pattern is chart-left / narrative-right at roughly 60:40,
each column with its own noun-phrase header — `Net profit/loss` … `Key drivers`;
`Work hours reduction` … `Sources of work hours reduction`.

**Bottom rail, fixed order**: `Note:` → numbered footnotes → blank → `SOURCE:`.
Page number bottom-right as `McKinsey & Company | 12`.

Source lines, verbatim — always the uppercase token, semicolon-separated, no period:

```
SOURCE: USPS; P.L. 109-435 (PAEA)
SOURCE: USPS 2009 10-K; USPS 2010 Budget
SOURCE: BCG; Global Insights; USPS Financial Forecast Model
SOURCE: OPM estimates; P.L. 109-435
```

## 3. The ghost deck

Titles alone tell the argument. Slide 2 is the governing assertion; 3 decomposes
it into exactly three drivers; 4–5 prove driver one, 6 proves driver two, 7–8
prove driver three.

**Announce-then-deliver**: `Four trends will affect postal economics going
forward` is followed by exactly four trend slides. `USPS can pursue two sets of
actions` is followed by exactly two.

**Repeat the frame, change the delta**: slides 18 and 27 use an identical
three-panel layout with different data, so the reader compares rather than
re-learns. Their callouts differ by one word — `Statutory debt ceiling of $15 B
will be reached in Oct 2010` becomes `…**still** reached in Oct 2010`.

The numeric spine lives entirely in the titles: `-$33B / $238B` (18) → `-$15B /
$115B` (27) → `remaining gap` (28).

## 4. Chart and data conventions

Every chart is a **plain-language title on line 1, a bare units line on line 2**:

```
Net profit/loss                              $ billions
Volume forecasts and actuals                 Billions of pieces
USPS workforce                               FTEs1, Thousands
Pieces per FTE                               Pieces per year, thousands
First-Class Mail, Standard Mail and GDP growth   Cumulative increase from 1973, percent
```

Note the last: the index base lives in the unit line, not the title.

- **CAGR on the line**: `+2.2% p.a.1`, with `1 Per Annum: Compound annual growth rate, 2010 to 2020`.
- **Actual vs forecast** split by a vertical divider labelled `Actual  Forecast`.
- **`~` is the universal hedge**: `~$2B`, `~$10B`, `~150 million delivery points`, `~ 4% per year`.
- **Ranges, not points, for uncertain forecasts**: `1.3-2.5` (Wages), `2.0-4.0` (Workers' Comp), against a reference line `Inflation (CPI at 1.9%)`.
- **Negatives**: minus signs in chart data (`-3.8`), parentheses in annotations (`($15B) FY2020`, `(1.5%) per year`).
- **Footnotes disclose method**, they are not asides: `Revenue declines calculating by applying 2009 prices against 2006-09 volume declines`; `Calculated by applying the 2009 First-Class/Standard mix to 2020 prices. Excludes mix shift in any other categories`.
- Superscript integers only, restarting at 1 each slide. No asterisks or daggers.

## 5. Callouts

Every callout carries a number or a named threshold. Never a generic label.

```
55% of reductions have come from non-career and overtime
Even if volumes remained flat instead of declining by 1.5% annually, the loss in 2020 would still be $21 billion
Statutory debt ceiling of $15 B will be reached in Oct 2010
An additional 27 Billion pieces are forecast to be lost (15% of total). Assumes no loss due to elasticity
USPS already processes 91% of mail through automation, the best in the world. Further improvements will be highly challenging
```

The slide-18 band is a **stress-test callout** — the pre-emptive answer to the
obvious objection.

## 6. Archetypes present

- **Agenda / section divider**, repeated verbatim four times (slides 1, 9, 20, 37).
- **Waterfall / bridge** ×3, all start bar → +/- deltas → end bar.
- **Sizing build-up**: slide 23 stacks `~$2B / ~$10B / ~$0.5B / ~$0.5B / ~$5B → Total ~$18B`, then slides 24–26 drill into each row and restate its subtotal.
- **Options taxonomy**: slide 30, five columns with coded IDs (`R1 R2 R3 P1 P2 P3 S1…`). Note the deck **screens but never scores** — there is no impact/feasibility 2×2 with plotted dots.
- **Screening funnel**: slide 31, inputs → longlist → rejects with explicit reject criteria.
- **Upside/downside pair**: slide 19, `Potential upside` | `Potential downside risks`.
- **Conceptual 2×2 with a centre insight box**: slides 10 and 13; every quadrant header carries a parenthetical scale figure (`Post Offices (36,500 Post Offices, Stations and Branches1)`).

**Absent** — do not claim these as McKinsey house style on this evidence: no
Marimekko, no scored 2×2 bubble matrix, no Gantt/roadmap, no scatter plot, no
standalone prose executive-summary slide, no competitor benchmark chart.
